Two Ways to Replace Bad Manual Skin

In both candidates we keep the manually corrected labels, except manual skin label 5. The difference is what fills the space after manual skin is removed.

manual non-skin labels kept skin/scalp CHARM fallback tissue risk: gap hidden as skin

Candidate A

Full CHARM fallback, then manual overlay
final = CHARM full tissue map final[manual label != 0 and manual label != 5] = manual label

Simple meaning: CHARM provides the whole backup head. We paste the trusted manual non-skin labels over it.

Implication: safest anatomically. If removing manual skin creates a gap, CHARM fills it with its own tissue labels instead of forcing it to skin.

Candidate B

Solid CHARM head mask labelled as skin, then manual overlay
solid_head = fill_holes(CHARM label > 0) final = background final[solid_head] = skin label 5 final[manual label != 0 and manual label != 5] = manual label

Simple meaning: CHARM provides only a solid head-shaped shell. We paste the trusted manual non-skin labels into it.

Implication: more aggressive. It can close holes, but it may turn unknown/gap regions into skin/scalp even if they should be skull or CSF.

What both candidates do Manual skin label 5 is thrown away. Manual non-skin labels are preserved.
The real difference Candidate A fills gaps with CHARM tissue labels. Candidate B fills gaps with skin.
Supervisor-friendly summary A is anatomically conservative. B is geometry-first and may over-label gap space as scalp.
